The Journey of Knowing

Not a teaching. Not a belief.
A quiet space to find what is already within you.

I can't explain my path, and I'm not meant to. Yours is your own.

Everything in this journey I have lived myself. I'm not here to teach you or tell you what to think.

There was a time when everything was pure freedom, pure love, and everything happened through the thought. I'm not there now, but the feeling, the energy, is still here.

This journey is a space. What you find in it was already yours.

Three crossings

Every path is personal. No one can walk it for you, so this journey doesn't describe it. It only lights it, one crossing at a time. You cross not by thinking you are ready, but by knowing.

The start step: The breath

Before Day 1, and at the start of every practice after it.

How each day works

  1. A question. Read it slowly. There is no right answer.
  2. A quiet practice. It always begins with the opening breath.
  3. A daily task. Something small to carry into your day.
  4. A reflection. A few words of your own. Only you will read them.

Don't try to get it right. Don't try to make anything happen. Relax, do the day's task, and let it come.

Why we begin with the breath

The human mind is covered in thought. Layer on layer: what we were taught, what we fear, what we expect, what we believe we already know. Under all that, it is hard to know anything at all.

In this journey's understanding, the pineal gland, a small gland deep in the centre of the head, is a window for inner knowing. When it is calcified, the window is covered, like glass under years of dust. What comes through it is no longer knowing. It is judgment. Quick labels: right, wrong, good, bad, true, nonsense.

Judgment feels like knowing. That is why it is so hard to see.

We can't clean the window by thinking about it. Thinking is more dust. We can't force it either.

We begin with the breath. It is always here, it needs no belief, and it happens now, never in the past or the future. Each slow breath settles a little of the dust. Not all at once. Day by day, the way water clears when you stop stirring it.

The opening breath

Breathe in gently through the nose for 3. Breathe out slowly for 6, long and soft, like a sigh with no sound. Nine breaths. Don't hold the breath and don't push. If the counts feel too long, make them shorter.

A long, slow out-breath tells the body it is safe. The body softens, and the mind follows.

Optional: if you can, breathe at dawn or dusk, when the light is changing, the times the forest monks sit.

The first task

Before Day 1, do the opening breath once today, and once more before sleep.

Reflection

Your reflections stay on this device only. No one else can read them.

A note on the body and science: the pineal gland is real. It makes melatonin, which helps us sleep and follow the rhythm of day and night. Calcification of the pineal gland is also real and is common, especially with age. Science has not shown that it causes judgment or blocks inner knowing. That part is this journey's spiritual understanding, offered for you to explore, not as medical fact.

A gentle note: go slowly. If a practice ever feels frightening or too much, stop, open your eyes and come back another day. This journey is not medical or mental-health care. If you are struggling, please reach out to someone you trust or a professional.

Day 1: The busy mind

Is it better to speak, or to stay quiet?

Most of us never ask. We speak because silence feels empty. We fill the quiet with words, with phones, with plans, with worry.

And then the mind asks its own questions: Why can't I find peace? Where is the answer? Why do I feel lost, even when everything is fine?

Today you don't need answers. Only notice how busy it is in there.

Quiet practice: 3 minutes

  1. Sit comfortably. Let your hands rest.
  2. Begin with the opening breath (nine breaths: in for 3, out for 6).
  3. For three minutes, simply notice your thoughts arriving. Don't stop them. Don't follow them. Just notice: a thought… another thought…
  4. When the three minutes end, take one more slow breath and open your eyes.

Daily task

Once today, when you are about to speak, pause first. Ask yourself silently: is this better spoken, or better left in silence? Then choose.

Reflection

Day 2: Listening, hearing, knowing

Have you ever listened to someone, and not heard them at all?

People listen but don't hear. Their mind is already somewhere else, preparing an answer, judging, waiting for their turn.

And we do it too.

There is a deeper kind of not-hearing: when we believe we already know. If I think I know, I stop listening. What I learned becomes a wall, and nothing new can come through.

Thinking you know and knowing are not the same.

Quiet practice: 6 minutes

  1. Sit comfortably. The opening breath.
  2. For six minutes, listen to the sounds around you: near, far, loud, quiet.
  3. Try not to name them ("car", "bird", "fan"). Just hear them, as if for the first time.
  4. If thoughts come, let them pass, and come back to listening.
  5. One slow breath. Open your eyes.

Daily task

In one conversation today, listen without preparing your answer. Let the other person finish completely before you think about what to say.

Reflection

Day 3: The Observer

When something enters your awareness, do you observe it, or do you straight away make it part of you?

You can be aware of something without picking it up. You can know someone's belief without making it yours, someone's anger or sadness without carrying it, a situation without becoming part of it, a thought without believing it.

Awareness does not mean taking part.
You can observe without absorbing.
You can hear without accepting.
You can see without judging.

This is a quiet kind of freedom. You are allowed to know that something exists, and still remain yourself.

Quiet practice: 9 minutes

  1. Sit comfortably. The opening breath.
  2. For nine minutes, watch your thoughts the way you might watch people walking past on a forest path. A thought comes. You see it. It walks on. You don't follow it down the path. You stay where you are.
  3. If you find you have followed one, that's fine. Notice, smile, and come back to where you sit.
  4. One slow breath. Open your eyes.

Daily task

Today, when you meet someone's strong feeling or opinion, notice it without picking it up. Silently say: I see it. I don't need to carry it.

Reflection

What comes after the crossing

Tomorrow is your first crossing. After it, if you choose, the journey goes deeper: Crossing 2, Laying the foundations.

The human mind has three deceptions: money, the body, and magic. Not sins, not mistakes. Only where the mind gets caught.

It begins with money, because money is the first thing the mind uses to tell us who we are. Not the lack of it, not the love of it: money itself.

The first crossing

You have walked three days. Three, then six, then nine minutes of stillness.

There is no test here. A test would check your thinking, and this journey is not about thinking.

Sit quietly once more. The opening breath. Then, in your own words:

And carry this question with you:

If I stop judging what I meet, what might I become aware of that I couldn't see before?

When you know you are ready, not when you think you should be, the next crossing is waiting.

Becoming: nine moons. Stillness, your birth day, beyond thought, the light, and the lotus.

You choose how to begin: follow the moon (the next new moon is), or begin the next morning. One step opens each morning.
